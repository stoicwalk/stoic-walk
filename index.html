About the tour

Not history.
A way of living.

Rome is where Stoic philosophy stopped being theory and became an art of living — a search for the good life. We walk together from the Portico d'Ottavia to the Capitoline Hill, through the same streets where Marcus Aurelius, Seneca and Epictetus asked the questions that still matter today.

This is not a lecture. Along the way, we practise — small exercises drawn from the Meditations and Seneca's Letters that help you think differently about stress, emotions, and what you can actually control.

"You have power over your mind, not outside events. Realise this, and you will find strength."

— Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

For those who want to bring home something more than a photograph.

The route

Four stops,
two thousand years.

I
Portico d'Ottavia
Where it all began
Zeno of Citium, 300 BC, a shipwreck, and a philosophy born under a porch. The origin of Stoicism — and the dichotomy of control.
II
Temple of Apollo
Time and fate
Seneca's radical obsession with time. "Everything belongs to others — time alone is ours." What are you postponing?
III
Theatre of Marcellus
The mask and the role
The Latin word persona means theatre mask. Epictetus on the roles we play — and the difference between playing them and being them.
IV
Capitoline Hill
The inner citadel
Marcus Aurelius governed the world and wrote notes to himself at night to stay human. The Meditations and the fortress within.

The philosophers

Three voices,
one question.

Epictetus
c. 50 – 135 AD

Born a slave in Hierapolis, freed after Nero's death. He had no physical freedom and no material possessions — and taught emperors how to be free. His Enchiridion is one of the most direct manuals for living ever written.

Seneca
c. 4 BC – 65 AD

One of the richest men in Rome, advisor to Nero, accused of hypocrisy. He never denied it: he said he was still learning. Ordered to take his own life, he did so calmly — dictating letters until the very last moment.

Marcus Aurelius
121 – 180 AD

Emperor of Rome at the height of its power. His Meditations were never meant to be published — they are private notes, a man reminding himself every night to stay humble, patient, and just. The most powerful man in the world, writing to himself.

Roma è il luogo dove la filosofia stoica ha smesso di essere teoria ed è diventata arte del vivere alla ricerca della felicità. Camminiamo insieme dal Portico d'Ottavia al Campidoglio, attraverso le stesse strade dove Marco Aurelio, Seneca ed Epitteto si facevano le domande che contano ancora oggi.

I filosofi

Tre voci,
una domanda.

Epitteto
c. 50 – 135 d.C.

Nato schiavo a Ierapoli, liberato dopo la morte di Nerone. Non aveva libertà fisica né beni materiali — e insegnava agli imperatori come essere liberi. Il suo Enchiridion è uno dei manuali di vita più diretti mai scritti.

Seneca
c. 4 a.C. – 65 d.C.

Uno degli uomini più ricchi di Roma, consigliere di Nerone, accusato di ipocrisia. Non lo negava mai: diceva che stava ancora imparando. Condannato a morte, lo fece con calma — dettando lettere fino all'ultimo momento.

Marco Aurelio
121 – 180 d.C.

Imperatore di Roma al culmine del suo potere. Le sue Meditazioni non erano destinate alla pubblicazione — sono appunti privati, un uomo che si ricorda ogni notte di restare umile, paziente e giusto. L'uomo più potente del mondo, che scriveva a sé stesso.

Les philosophes

Trois voix,
une seule question.

Épictète
c. 50 – 135 ap. J.-C.

Né esclave à Hiérapolis, affranchi après la mort de Néron. Il n'avait ni liberté physique ni biens matériels — et enseignait aux empereurs comment être libres. Son Enchiridion est l'un des manuels de vie les plus directs jamais écrits.

Sénèque
c. 4 av. J.-C. – 65 ap. J.-C.

L'un des hommes les plus riches de Rome, conseiller de Néron, accusé d'hypocrisie. Il ne le niait jamais: il disait qu'il apprenait encore. Condamné à mort, il s'exécuta calmement — dictant des lettres jusqu'au dernier moment.

Marc Aurèle
121 – 180 ap. J.-C.

Empereur de Rome à l'apogée de sa puissance. Ses Méditations n'étaient pas destinées à être publiées — ce sont des notes privées, un homme se rappelant chaque nuit de rester humble, patient et juste. L'homme le plus puissant du monde, s'écrivant à lui-même.
