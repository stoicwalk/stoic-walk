THE ROUTE Four stops, two thousand years. I Portico d'Ottavia Where it all began Zeno of Citium, 300 BC, a shipwreck, and a philosophy born under a porch. The origin of Stoicism — and the dichotomy of control. II Temple of Apollo Time and fate Seneca's radical obsession with time. "Everything belongs to others — time alone is ours." What are you postponing? III Theatre of Marcellus The mask and the role The Latin word persona means theatre mask. Epictetus on the roles we play — and the difference between playing them and being them. IV Capitoline Hill The inner citadel Marcus Aurelius governed the world and wrote notes to himself at night to stay human. The Meditations and the fortress within. THE PHILOSOPHERS Three voices, one question. Epictetus c. 50 – 135 AD Born a slave in Hierapolis, freed after Nero's death. He had no physical freedom and no material possessions — and taught emperors how to be free. His Enchiridion is one of the most direct manuals for living ever written. Seneca c. 4 BC – 65 AD One of the richest men in Rome, advisor to Nero, accused of hypocrisy. He never denied it: he said he was still learning. Ordered to take his own life, he did so calmly — dictating letters until the very last moment. Marcus Aurelius 121 – 180 AD Emperor of Rome at the height of its power. His Meditations were never meant to be published — they are private notes, a man reminding himself every night to stay humble, patient, and just.
